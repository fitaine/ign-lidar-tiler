IGN LiDAR Tiler

Use this when you have nothing yet. You pick an area of France on the map, and it downloads the LiDAR tiles under it, fetches the aerial photo for colour, and builds one light point cloud you can actually move around in Blender.

What you get: a scene folder with the tiles, a scene.json that remembers where everything is, and a .ply to import. Light on purpose — lighting and framing come first, density comes last, in stage 3.

Use this for an old scene the app has never seen. Anything you built before this app existed: the .blend is lit and carved, but there is no scene.json, so the app has no idea which square kilometre of France it belongs to.

You point at roughly where it is, and it matches the shape of your terrain against the real ground to find the exact spot — then downloads the tiles and writes the scene.json that stage 3 needs. Your .blend is not modified.

Use this when the look is finished and you are ready to render. It takes the .blend you lit and framed, throws away its light cloud, and puts a dense one in its place — as many points as your graphics card can hold.

Your own .blend is never touched. The result is a separate -HEADLESS.blend for rendering from the command line only: far too heavy to open normally.
